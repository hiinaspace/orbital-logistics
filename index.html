About the project

A place to watch orbital motion.

Part orrery, part operating model railway. The interest is in the journeys and the changing views of them.

What am I looking at?

Prepared, fictional space-traffic exhibits: ships carrying freight, meeting stations, launching, landing and riding between worlds. There are no objectives to complete. Select a set and look around.

How much physics is simulated?

Ships follow three-dimensional Newtonian trajectories under the gravity of prescribed moving bodies. Powered flights can model finite thrust, steering, acceleration limits and fuel use. Some exhibits also include staging, stationkeeping and simplified atmospheric drag and lift.

This is not a fully interacting N-body system: ships do not pull on one another, the large bodies follow specified paths, and docking uses bounded position and velocity capture rather than hull contact. The atmosphere and vehicles are schematic.

Are these real missions or realistic scales?

The systems are deliberately small enough to read as an orrery. Distances, body sizes, engines and schedules are chosen to make the movement visible. Apollo, Webb and proposed cyclers are inspirations, rather than mission reconstructions.

Is everything calculated live?

Some journeys are prepared in advance; continuous services combine reusable trajectories with bounded planning as traffic runs. Both use the same underlying flight dynamics. The small preview at the top of this page is a repeating recording of prepared positions, without the viewer or its planners.

What do the frames and gravity maps show?

Frames change the origin and orientation of your view, not a ship’s flight. The potential map shows a planar slice of the gravitational landscape. Relief bends that slice into a schematic rubber sheet; its height is a visual aid, not an extra physical dimension the ships move through.

What inspired it?

The maneuver views of Children of a Dead Earth, the small worlds of Outer Wilds, NASA’s Eyes, and the pleasure of watching an operating model railway.

How is it built?

TypeScript and Vite, with a Canvas 2D schematic viewer and optional WebGL relief. Numerical engines run independently of the interface; workers handle the heavier preparation and planning. Shared playback can feed two independently controlled views.

Does it work on a phone?

This page is built to be lightweight and readable on a small screen. The full viewer currently works best on a desktop with a mouse and keyboard; its mobile controls are still a future pass.
