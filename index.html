Orbital Logistics

An orrery with spaceships

Hiina · Open viewer ↗

This is a model railway for spaceships: small fictional worlds, with freighters, landers and stations going about their day.

There is nothing to build or win. You can follow a ship, change your point of view, slow down for a burn, or leave the traffic running. Explore the exhibits, or read on for a few views of what is inside.

One flight, different frames

Much of the fun has been finding another way to look at the same journey. A path that seems tangled in an inertial frame can resolve into a simple loop when the view rotates with a planet and its moon. The ships have not changed course; only the observer has moved.

Each view has its own camera and selection, but they share a clock. You can also keep a system-wide overview beside a chase camera, or compare top and front views of an orbit. Playback runs forwards or backwards, and the activity lanes let you scrub to a particular departure or encounter.

A gravity landscape

For the coplanar sets, a potential map adds contours beneath the trajectories. The relief view bends that map into a rubber sheet. I like how this makes the space between the bodies feel like a landscape, rather than an empty background.

This is a schematic height map of a planar slice of the potential. Its vertical scale is adjustable; the ships are not physically climbing and sliding along a surface.

Some places to start

The sets explore different kinds of motion and different scales:

  • Cycler fleet: carriers loop past two worlds while smaller shuttles ferry freight between them and the ground. Inspired by the idea of a lunar cycler.
  • Full service: a busier neighborhood of staged expeditions, lunar landers, ground ports and relay stations.
  • Continuous survey: an observatory traces a three-dimensional Lissajous orbit near L2. Long trails gradually reveal its pattern.

About the simulation

How much physics is simulated?

Ships follow three-dimensional Newtonian trajectories under the gravity of prescribed moving bodies. Powered flights can model finite thrust, steering, acceleration limits and fuel use. Some exhibits also include staging, stationkeeping and simplified atmospheric drag and lift.

The large bodies follow specified paths, and ships do not pull on one another. Docking uses bounded position and velocity capture rather than hull contact. Vehicles and atmospheres are schematic.

Scale and missions

These are fictional systems, deliberately small enough to read as an orrery. Distances, body sizes, engines and schedules are chosen to make the movement interesting to watch. Apollo, Webb and proposed cyclers are inspirations, rather than mission reconstructions.

Prepared and live trajectories

Some journeys are prepared in advance; continuous services combine reusable trajectories with planning as traffic runs. Both use the same underlying flight dynamics. The small animation at the top of this page plays back prepared positions without loading the viewer or its planners.

Implementation

The viewer is built with TypeScript and Vite, Canvas 2D for the schematic views and WebGL for relief. The numerical engines are independent of the interface, with heavier preparation and planning running in workers. The full viewer currently works best on a desktop with a mouse and keyboard.

Inspiration

The maneuver views of Children of a Dead Earth, the small worlds of Outer Wilds, NASA’s Eyes, and the pleasure of watching an operating model railway.
