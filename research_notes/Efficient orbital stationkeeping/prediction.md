# Efficient stationkeeping, sensitivity and prediction

## Can analytic sensitivities replace repeated finite-difference trajectory integrations?

### Takeaway

Yes: retaining shooting but propagating control sensitivities with one nominal trajectory is the most direct method change to investigate. It preserves the finite pulse family and admitted physical programs; it does not require introducing a new continuously reacting controller. The speedup must be measured, because augmented integration adds arithmetic even while eliminating repeated nonlinear trajectory evaluations.

### Cited Findings

- York and Howell's **A Two-Level Differential Corrections Algorithm for Low-Thrust Spacecraft Trajectory Targeting** (2019), pp. 2–6, describes Newton correction using a constraint Jacobian, STMs obtained by propagation or finite differences, and STM composition across thrust/coast arcs. Its finite-burn formulation includes mass and thrust parameters. It explicitly distinguishes design-stage variables from the smaller set of physically available in-flight controls. Changing burn end time requires a term involving the difference between accelerations before and after the switch; holding all switch epochs fixed simplifies the problem. — [Author-hosted paper](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf)
- Orekit's published CR3BP differential corrector integrates additional STM equations alongside the orbit and uses their entries to correct initial conditions. Its plane-crossing target has an event-time correction involving terminal acceleration and crossing velocity. This is working numerical-library code rather than a proposal, although its halo-orbit problem differs from this exhibit. — [Orekit source](https://www.orekit.org/site-orekit-10.3.1/xref/org/orekit/orbits/CR3BPDifferentialCorrection.html)
- NASA's Q-Law enhancement project reports deriving analytic thrust-vector partials, using them to form STMs, and obtaining better optimization efficiency and accuracy than finite-difference solutions. This supports analytic derivatives as an established approach, but supplies no transferable browser-performance factor. — [NASA TechPort project 118434](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/118434)
- This application's accepted relay controller already freezes two constant-force 1.9-unit pulses separated by 0.2 units, with phase-free reference geometry. Coupled service includes fuel consumption, attached visitor mass and exact donor transfer, so control sensitivities must include those dependencies. — [Stationkeeping contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md); [Flexible-service contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md)

### Inferences

- **Own derivation for the stated force model:** for prescribed point-mass positions `b_i(t)`, let `d_i = r - b_i(t)`. Gravity has Jacobian

  `G = Σ μ_i [3 d_i d_iᵀ / |d_i|⁵ - I / |d_i|³]`.

  For six-state dynamics `y = (r,v)`, the gravity-only state Jacobian is `A = [[0,I],[G,0]]`. For control parameters `p`, integrate `S = ∂y/∂p` with `S' = A S + ∂f/∂p`. This carries the six control directions together instead of running six separate perturbed trajectories. Prescribed primary/secondary positions need no spacecraft-dependent derivative. This follows the sensitivity structure used by [York and Howell](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf), specialized here by direct differentiation of the stated gravity law.
- The useful first target is **control sensitivity**, not necessarily a full generic state-transition framework. The existing six force components need six columns; if only fewer directions matter, propagate only those. A full 6×6 STM does not automatically buy a sevenfold speedup: it integrates 36 extra values, but its matrix operations avoid many repeated expensive force evaluations. [NASA's analytic-derivative project](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/118434) establishes the method, not a timing prediction for this code.
- Differentiate **the executed RK4 step map**, including its stage states and exact anchored mass evaluation. RK4 integration of correctly staged variational equations can provide this derivative while following the identical fixed step schedule. A continuous-flow derivative computed with a different integrator is a good approximate Newton direction, but does not itself certify the canonical endpoint. This is a numerical design recommendation motivated by [Orekit's joint orbit/STM propagation](https://www.orekit.org/site-orekit-10.3.1/xref/org/orekit/orbits/CR3BPDifferentialCorrection.html) and the [repository's exact execution contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- Fuel has a derivative too: under a force-limited pulse, `a = F/m(t,p)` implies both direct force sensitivity and `-F m_p/m²`. Acceleration caps, fuel exhaustion and donor/attachment events introduce branches. Use branch-consistent derivatives and exact boundaries, and fall back to feasible one-sided checks when crossing an active set. A sensitivity that assumes constant mass will be wrong when changing force changes propellant use, or when the visitor joins the station. [Finite-burn mass model](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf); [coupled ownership rules](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- At fixed ownership epochs, compose local sensitivity maps and event reset derivatives instead of differentiating an entire mutable world blindly. If a visitor target depends on the same planned station controls, differentiate the **relative endpoint residual**, including station motion. Reusing only the visitor derivative silently treats a changing target as fixed. [STM arc composition](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf); [frozen coupled dependencies](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).

### Gaps

- No measured speedup for this application's RK4, allocation patterns or canonical event splitting. A bounded comparison should record force calls, Jacobian rebuilds, accepted/rejected Newton steps and wall time separately.
- This research does not establish that replacing finite differences alone resolves head stalls: repeated guide-controller solves, candidate enumeration and final proof may dominate after that change.

## Can stationkeeping itself be cheaper than solving a precise six-state rendezvous every interval?

### Takeaway

Real stationkeeping commonly controls a small set of orbit characteristics and allows other state components or phase to drift. That suggests reducing the relay maintenance objective or using local feedback, while still solving visitor docking against the actual paid trajectory. A Gateway controller cannot be copied directly onto a primary-centered forced circle.

### Cited Findings

- Davis, McCarthy and Zimovan-Spreen's **Perturbations and Recovery in the Gateway Near Rectilinear Halo Orbit** (AAS 23-190), pp. 2–3, describes low-cost targeting of one rotating velocity component several revolutions downstream. That keeps the spacecraft in the orbit but lets phase vary. Adding a perilune-time target maintains phase; adding position temporarily damps deviations after perturbations. The authors explicitly say six-element reference-state targeting is prohibitively expensive for long-term operations and verify other orbital characteristics because a single target admits many trajectories. Their expense statement concerns operational maneuvering, not a benchmark of solver CPU time. — [NASA paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220019129/downloads/AAS_Austin_DavisSpreenMcCarthy_strives_v2.pdf)
- **Orbit Maintenance and Navigation of Human Spacecraft at Cislunar Near Rectilinear Halo Orbits** (2017), pp. 5–7, separates long-horizon reference construction from short-horizon maintenance because that separation is computationally advantageous. It notes that keeping a non-ballistic reference costs more propellant and recommends a nearly ballistic continuous reference for low-cost maintenance. — [NASA paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170001347/downloads/20170001347.pdf)
- Zuehlke et al.'s **Rendezvous and Proximity Operations in Cislunar Space Using Linearized Dynamics for Estimation** (2023) demonstrates a linear relative-motion matrix-exponential model with LQR. The chief orbit remains nonlinear; EXPM avoids integrating the 36 STM components. The discrete LQR gain comes from a Riccati equation, and online control is `u = -Kx`. It is a proof of concept in a specified CR3BP relative-motion scenario, not a general transfer solution. — [Author research article](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/8/674)
- The repository already permits physical relay phase drift, uses position/velocity deadbands and an envelope, and distinguishes missed maintenance from collision/fault. That is compatible with a lower-dimensional maintenance objective in principle; changing the objective or feedback cadence would require a new policy with evidence. — [Existing maintenance policy](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md)

### Inferences

- A phase-free radius-3 circle is a **one-dimensional orbit manifold** in six-state space. Its tangent phase direction should not be penalized as a clock error. Consider circularization errors such as radius, radial velocity, angular momentum/plane and tangential speed, or project the state residual perpendicular to the manifold tangent. The current phase projection is already part of that contract; its derivative must be included when generating a Newton Jacobian. The NASA result supports controlling orbit characteristics, but the correct residuals for this circle are an application design decision. [NASA maintenance distinction](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220019129/downloads/AAS_Austin_DavisSpreenMcCarthy_strives_v2.pdf); [phase projection definition](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md).
- A sampled LQR/PD-style controller could choose pulse corrections from measured orbit errors without a nonlinear solve on every block. Gains can be scheduled on orbital/perturber phase and loaded mass. This trades solver work for controller design, envelopes and saturation handling. A constant gain tuned around one phase is not assured across changing perturbations and attachment mass. [LQR/EXPM precedent](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/8/674); [ownership changes](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- Feedback needs a physically achievable nominal law or feedforward compensation: the desired exact two-body circle is not a free solution of the perturbed field. A controller that treats it as ballistic may accumulate bias or pay excessive correction. A reference following more of the natural perturbed motion may reduce fuel, but changes the exhibit's intended geometry. [NASA reference continuity finding](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170001347/downloads/20170001347.pdf).
- Continuous feedback would be a **new operating-physics policy**. Rendering/scrubbing cannot trigger it. Choose gains and commands on canonical epochs, include fuel/caps/loaded mass, then freeze the paid finite segments that are used by docking. A cheap feedback or matrix-exponential **preview** can suggest those commands; it cannot substitute approximate positions for physical docking targets. [Current admission contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- Do not import NRHO unstable-mode or `vx` rules verbatim. Those rely on different orbit geometry, crossing symmetry, stability and a near-natural reference; this relay is a periodically actuated primary-centered orbit under a prescribed moving secondary. The transferable idea is selective control of orbit characteristics. [NASA's specific NRHO construction](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220019129/downloads/AAS_Austin_DavisSpreenMcCarthy_strives_v2.pdf).

### Gaps

- No source proves a particular LQR/PD gain, cadence or orbit-error envelope for the stated dimensionless relay system.
- Maintaining phase-free geometry and solving six-state **visitor capture** are different problems. No cited maintenance method proves capture to the repository's strict canonical residual tolerance.

## Should lower-fidelity planning or receding-horizon/MPC replace the expensive targeter?

### Takeaway

Use cheaper approximations to generate and rank controls, then bounded nonlinear/canonical correction and full admission. MPC is useful when online constraints and disturbances need repeated adjustment, but it is not an automatic performance cure and it would expand the current frozen-plan contract.

### Cited Findings

- Newman et al.'s **Stationkeeping, Orbit Determination, and Attitude Control for Spacecraft in Near Rectilinear Halo Orbits** (2018), pp. 3–4, extends maintenance to finite burns, yet finds finite-burn targeting unnecessary for its apolune NRHO maintenance scenario: it increases compute without reducing maintenance cost. The algorithm solves an impulsive correction, uses the rocket equation to derive burn duration for the stack mass, and executes a finite burn. The analysis includes changes in docked stack mass and operational perturbations. This approximation is justified for that loose orbit-maintenance target, not exact docking. — [NASA paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180006800/downloads/20180006800.pdf)
- **Chance-constrained Model Predictive Control for Near Rectilinear Halo Orbit Spacecraft Rendezvous**, available as an author preprint, uses a linear time-varying close-relative-motion model, finite electric controls parameterized by B-splines, and quadratic programs with control/path constraints. It validates using nonlinear relative dynamics and explicitly limits applicability to close rendezvous where separation is small relative to distances to the primaries. It proposes computing STMs on the ground in advance. Reported continuous-thrust computation on an i7-860 is 6.3504 seconds for stack matrices, 2.0369 seconds mean per robust MPC step and 3.5514 seconds worst observed. These are contextual timings, not predictions for modern hardware or this browser. — [Author preprint full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10437v1)
- The existing application already ranks schedules with cheap Lambert/Hohmann seeds, then admits bounded coupled flight/station/ownership plans. Those seeds never execute. A final coupled reconstruction has independent limits and the logical head waits for the next controller/admission. — [Current prediction/execution boundary](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md)

### Inferences

- **Recommended order:** retain finite pulses and ownership first; add analytic canonical control sensitivities; reuse previous converged controls/Jacobians as initial guesses with bounded refresh; increase cheap-model screening only when it reduces counted canonical work. Only then evaluate reduced maintenance objectives or a new feedback policy. This minimizes simultaneous semantic changes. [Sensitivity precedent](https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/118434); [current admission boundary](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- The NASA impulsive-to-finite result offers a strong stationkeeping seed strategy. It does **not** license impulsive execution or trusting the seed endpoint. With `μ₁=1, R=3`, the two-body period is about 32.65 units; the four-unit active portion occupies about 12% of a revolution. These supplied-model calculations suggest measuring the finite-duration discrepancy rather than assuming the entire active interval is negligible. [NASA approximation scope](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180006800/downloads/20180006800.pdf); [accepted pulse family](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md).
- Build a small local linear model around a canonical station/visitor guide, reuse it to rank timing/pulse candidates, and refresh only when the guide changes materially. The EXPM or STM-based preview remains valid only locally; long origin-to-relay flights need nonlinear correction. [EXPM precedent](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/8/674); [close-range MPC restriction](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10437v1).
- A lookup table is not keyed by block index alone. The relay phase is free, the secondary keeps moving, tanks deplete, and visitor attachments change acceleration. Cache keys should contain relevant relative phase, physical anchor, mass/engine state and control/event topology; a nearby key supplies a seed, never a certified endpoint. [Repository phase and ownership contracts](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).
- Linear MPC can explicitly handle constrained pulses where LQR needs saturation logic, but force-sphere bounds, fuel/attachment dependencies and far-transfer nonlinearities complicate the cheap quadratic form. Nonlinear MPC reintroduces repeated rollout/derivative work. The cited seconds-per-step example is concrete evidence that the label “MPC” alone does not imply interactive throughput. [MPC formulation and timings](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10437v1).
- Canonical admission must still verify both visitor legs, every funded loaded-station segment, actual endpoints, paid residual capture, clearance and donor accounting. Receding-horizon changes cannot modify a station future on which an already admitted visit depends. Short committed heads plus longer private guesses are compatible with the existing runtime, but require a dependency-aware promotion boundary. [Coupled-service contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md).

### Gaps

- No reviewed source establishes a guaranteed interactive budget for this exact finite-pulse coupled fixture. One optimized full validation may still be expensive even after planning becomes cheap.
- The MPC paper bounds acceleration, not this application's complete variable-mass donor/receiver event system; its equations do not establish fuel/ownership conservation here.
- No implementation, new controller tuning, source-code profiling or performance benchmark was performed in this research subtask. Recommendations are migration hypotheses for a measured experiment, not a claim that simulation head stalls are resolved.
