# Efficient near-circular orbital stationkeeping with elements and vectors

## Do real GEO/LEO controllers avoid optimizing orbital phase?

### Takeaway
Element-based control often makes the planning problem much cheaper, but phase is a mission requirement, not something all real controllers discard. GEO must hold longitude and many LEO missions must hold a ground track or formation separation; this fictional relay is explicitly phase-free, so its maintenance controller can omit phase error while retaining exact physical phase for rendezvous.

### Cited Findings
- Losa et al. (2005), *Electric Station Keeping of Geostationary Satellites: a Differential Inclusion Approach*, use nonsingular equinoctial variables: semimajor axis, eccentricity components `e cos(ω+Ω), e sin(ω+Ω)`, inclination components `tan(i/2) cosΩ, tan(i/2) sinΩ`, and mean longitude. They explain why perigee/node angles become inconvenient at very small eccentricity/inclination. Their mission constraint is a longitude/latitude box, and their planner is a constrained linear time-varying quadratic optimization, not Cartesian nonlinear shooting. See §§II–IV. — [Author paper in conference proceedings](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/cdc-ecc05/pdffiles/papers/0916.pdf)
- Gaias and D’Amico (2015), *Impulsive Maneuvers for Formation Reconfiguration Using Relative Orbital Elements*, describe simple closed-form Gauss-equation control used by SAFE and TAFF. TAFF uses pairs of along-track impulses separated by half a revolution. Their relative elements include relative longitude and semimajor-axis drift because formation geometry requires them. See pp.1037–1038, Eqs.(2)–(3). — [Author paper, Stanford Space Rendezvous Laboratory](https://slab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj25201/files/media/file/jgcd2015_gaiasdamico.pdf)
- Gurfil (2024), *Milankovitch–Lyapunov Geostationary Satellite Stationkeeping*, derives angular-momentum/eccentricity regulation first, then treats longitude control separately. Its unperturbed stability derivation starts with perturbation `p=0`; the published perturbed demonstrations are Earth GEO cases. — [Author-uploaded paper, DOI 10.2514/1.G008263](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382120856_Milankovitch-Lyapunov_Geostationary_Satellite_Stationkeeping)

### Inferences
- Recommended maintenance objective: regulate orbital size/energy, eccentricity vector and plane normal, with no desired longitude or anomaly. In a coplanar fixture this reduces to three useful scalar targets: `a−3, e_x, e_y`. If using Cartesian vector invariants, target `h_d=(0,0,sqrt(3))`, `e_d=0` for prograde circular motion with `μ=1`. Keeping both `a` and `h` as independently hard constraints is redundant when `e=0`; choose one size measure for conditioning.
- This drops a terminal constraint, not a state variable. Propagate the actual phase in the canonical six-state trajectory. Visitors target that committed moving station, including its actual position, velocity and finite burns. A maintained orbit need not return to a predetermined angular position each eight-unit interval.
- Merely changing a six-dimensional residual into elements will not eliminate the stall if every residual/Jacobian evaluation still propagates a complete nonlinear finite-burn episode. The saving comes from algebraic feedback, analytic response matrices, fewer constrained outputs, deadbands, bounded refinement, and predictable fallback.

### Gaps
- The cited real demonstrations do not establish a fuel-optimal policy for this fictional phase-free orbit with its close moving secondary. No measured speedup for this repository has been established in this research pass.

## What deadbands, mean elements and efficient corrections are useful?

### Takeaway
The strongest transferable idea is to control a permissible orbit family rather than cancel every instantaneous deviation. Mean-element control avoids spending propellant on harmless periodic oscillation; a small analytic element-response solve can propose corrections, while canonical execution certifies actual physical bounds.

### Cited Findings
- De Bruijn (2017), *Guidance Control and Dynamics of a New Generation of Geostationary Satellites*, §2.4 pp.40–44, argues for compensating secular drift while allowing periodic effects that fit operational limits. Mean elements remove selected periodic terms; an orbital-period average removes orbital-period terms, and longer periods require a different averaging interval. Constraints on mean elements must include margin for mean-to-osculating excursions. — [DLR thesis](https://elib.dlr.de/112638/1/dissertation_deBruijn.pdf)
- Arnas (2020), *Linearized Model for Satellite Station-Keeping and Tandem Formations under the Effects of Atmospheric Drag*, develops analytic deadband timing and correction magnitudes for near-circular LEO under simplified drag. Its along-track maneuver period scales with the square root of the allowed band divided by decay rate; this illustrates deriving cadence from drift and tolerance rather than solving every instant. See §3.3, Eq.(41). — [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.00285v1)
- Gurfil’s discrete implementation applies acceleration thresholds to the algebraic controller, leaving thrust off below threshold; it is an acceleration-command deadband, distinct from an orbit-error box. See §IV.B, Eq.(33). The element calculations use `h=r×v`, `e=(v×h)/μ−r/|r|`, directly from physical state. — [Author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382120856_Milankovitch-Lyapunov_Geostationary_Satellite_Stationkeeping)
- Gaias and D’Amico’s near-circular impulse map makes correction dependence on burn location explicit: `Δa/a=2Δv_T/(na)`, `Δe_x=(Δv_R sin u+2Δv_T cos u)/(na)`, `Δe_y=(−Δv_R cos u+2Δv_T sin u)/(na)`. Its normal-impulse response is `Δi_x=Δv_N cos u/(na)`, `Δi_y=Δv_N sin u/(na)`. These are linearized relative-element relations, not exact finite-burn propagation. — [Author paper, Eq.(3)](https://slab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj25201/files/media/file/jgcd2015_gaiasdamico.pdf)
- Leomanni et al., *Autonomous Low Earth Orbit Station-Keeping*, compute an osculating reference trajectory by numerical integration of the physical force model; initial mean elements are converted to osculating elements first. See guidance §B.1, Eqs.(46)–(47). — [Author paper](https://www3.diism.unisi.it/~control/Aerospace/papers/GCD11.pdf)

### Inferences
- A cheap candidate planner can evaluate an uncontrolled short forecast once, compute its ending element drift, and solve a small bounded linear least-squares correction using element response columns. With frozen burn windows, integrate the response along the reference orbit over each finite pulse (quadrature of the Gauss response multiplied by available acceleration), rather than pretending a long burn is an instantaneous impulse. Exact moving-secondary acceleration can enter that reference forecast/feedforward. One canonical candidate admission then supplies trustworthy position, velocity, fuel and safety.
- A still simpler candidate is algebraic `h/e` feedback sampled on predetermined canonical control intervals. Freeze those commands into an inspectable program before forecasting/admitting a coupled visit. It needs no repeated nonlinear terminal solve, but may cost more fuel than well-timed burns and changes the present two-pulse control contract if implemented as many intervals.
- Use an outer orbit-quality band plus a stricter predictive trigger: coast if the forecast remains inside the band through the next usable burn opportunity; otherwise move toward the interior. Hysteresis and a minimum dwell/decision interval prevent tiny oscillations from continually re-triggering planning. This is an engineering recommendation, not a theorem established for this fixture.
- Do not label an arbitrary eight-unit rolling average as canonical mean elements: the unperturbed orbital period is `2πsqrt(3³)=32.6484`, so eight units span only 24.5% of an orbit. A causal filter also introduces delay. For this application, bounded osculating-element envelopes with short exact disturbance forecast may be simpler and more honest than importing an elaborate GEO averaging theory.
- Deadband targets should be orbital quality/safety/service availability, not zero numerical rendezvous residual. Landing/capture admission remains exact and paid; maintenance can permit a visibly near-circular range whose actual trajectory visitors rendezvous with.

### Gaps
- Deadband widths and correction cadence need fixture-specific fuel, stability and rendezvous-availability measurements. The real-world days/years and thrust values cannot be transferred into these dimensionless model units.
- An analytic feedforward table over secondary-relative phase might amortize repeated operation, but reproducibility across fuel and attachment mass requires validation; no such table was measured here.

## Which assumptions break with the nearby moving perturber and attached mass?

### Takeaway
Near-circular element control remains a useful local coordinate choice, but Earth GEO secular formulas, distant-third-body expansions and impulse timing cannot be copied as a certified controller. Keep exact canonical dynamics and mass/fuel ownership as the admission authority.

### Cited Findings
- De Bruijn’s third-body approximation is proportional to `μ_3 r/s³ (−r_hat+3 s_hat(s_hat·r_hat))`; his linear GEO approximations are justified for a small slot around a prescribed station and physical Sun/Moon geometry. His selected mean-to-osculating terms depend on which periodic excursions fit that slot. See §§2.3–2.4, Eq.(2.24). — [DLR thesis](https://elib.dlr.de/112638/1/dissertation_deBruijn.pdf)
- Losa et al. map thrust force to acceleration with a spacecraft-mass factor `1/m`; changing available force, geometry or mass changes the control bounds even when the orbital-coordinate equations remain useful. See Eq.(48). — [Author paper](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/cdc-ecc05/pdffiles/papers/0916.pdf)

### Inferences
- Model-specific calculations from the supplied assignment: relay radius/secondary radius is `3/6=0.5`. A distant-third-body tidal/quadrupole expansion is not a controlled small-ratio approximation here. At alignment the relay-secondary separation is 3 and direct secondary acceleration is `.004/3²=.0004444`, versus primary `.111111`: 0.4%. At opposite alignment separation is 9 and the ratio is 0.0444%. The perturbation is locally modest compared with central gravity, yet varies substantially around the orbit; “strong” should refer to this departure from the simplifying geometry, not dominance over primary acceleration.
- If the code holds the primary fixed and adds direct secondary gravity, its force convention differs from the usual freely falling primary-centered differential third-body acceleration. Copying an indirect subtraction from Earth-centered literature would change the fictional model. Verify the actual force convention before using any feedforward.
- Assuming the secondary’s angular rate is the primary-only circular rate, its period is `2πsqrt(6³)=92.3436`; relay-secondary synodic period is approximately 50.5. Disturbance phase evolves meaningfully over an eight-unit forecast; a frozen or orbit-averaged force cannot be assumed adequate. These periods depend on the actual secondary rail convention and should be checked against the fixture.
- The first four units cover at most `4/sqrt(27)=0.7698 rad≈44.1°` of nominal orbital angle. Thus the current two pulses cannot exploit the half-revolution separation used by TAFF. Two nearly co-located response columns can be poorly conditioned for certain eccentricity-vector corrections; adding radial authority or adjusting time windows may be more effective than iterating a bad solve.
- With thrust `.06` and acceleration cap `.012`, the cap transition occurs at total propelled mass 5: below it the acceleration limit binds, above it force/mass binds. Attachment transfers change response and burn duration. Fuel depletion makes mass evolve continuously; the planner must include actual donor/recipient fuel and carried visitor dry/cargo/fuel mass, and must not reuse a mass-specific response blindly.
- Saturating or restricting an algebraic controller to two finite pulses does not inherit Gurfil’s unsaturated unperturbed stability result. Use it as a candidate generator, then validate long-horizon envelope, fuel, capped acceleration, event boundaries and coupled visit/return admission in the existing canonical Runner.
- Recommended first experiment: a phase-free three-output element residual plus analytic finite-pulse response, exact short disturbance forecast, and a fixed small refinement budget. Compare wall-clock cost, worst orbit-quality envelope, fuel, admitted service count and bounded failure behavior against the existing shooting planner. Preserve committed forecasts and rendezvous state accuracy; if the cheap candidate fails admission, defer/retry a window within explicit limits.

### Gaps
- No cited result proves the proposed controller for a fixed-primary direct-force model with fuel-dependent acceleration, paid capture, attachment windows and changing carried mass. Those are repository-specific validation obligations.
- This research does not recommend replacing canonical RK4 with averaged dynamics. Accurate physical rendezvous requires osculating state reconstruction or exact integrated state, regardless of the coordinates used for planning.
