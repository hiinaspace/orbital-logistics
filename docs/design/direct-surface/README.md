# Direct surface-flight comparison

The owner rejected the choreography of the prepared-surface/live-relay exhibit.
Its numerically continuous radius-two circular interfaces still impose two distinct
flight families and a local/orbital engine-mode switch. This is a trajectory
authorship issue worth isolating before deciding whether caching itself is useful.

The new finite **Orison · Direct surface flight** keeps Equator, Meridian, Mote,
Tern's mass/tank/exhaust velocity, and the existing surface engine ceiling/cap.
Each complete free-flight leg is integrated in the full Orison–Mote field:
gravity-turn burn, coast, rendezvous burn; deorbit burn, coast, terminal braking.
There is no prescribed circular intermediate orbit or geometry-cache handoff.
The same 32 force ceiling / 3 acceleration cap applies throughout each flight.
The finite exhibit uses that surface-capable engine for the complete route,
rather than switching to the cached exhibit's .8 / .4 orbital operating mode.

An offline primary-only radius-three boundary-value study supplies **steering and
duration seeds**, not runtime trajectories. Preparation solves four planar
endpoint constraints by adjusting two steering keys and two burn durations.
Every trial integrates the actual epoch-dependent field and fuel law. Trials may
miss the surface; they are never admitted. Both final legs replay with the real
collision sphere and unchanged Runner clearance certification. Full six-state
paid endpoint matches must remain below 2e-8 position/velocity and fuel reserve
must exceed .05. Ground support and station attachment are finite-exhibit
lifecycle states; free flight uses normal Runner programs without relaxing the
old one-primary surface-port schema.

Meridian retains the existing controller and explicit capture/top-up/release mass
ownership. A supplied ground-pose provider replaces only the otherwise ballistic
origin; fork preserves it. The fixture's early arrival finds a full station tank,
so its computed top-up is zero. A separate longer-visit test exercises actual
maintenance while Tern is attached. Station propulsion inventory is displayed
from its authoritative physical owner instead of treating a top-up as a second
depot stock.

The viewer prepares one finite history in its worker, then permits playback,
rewind, scrubbing, frames, 3D, Follow/Chase, and Replay. It starts just before
launch at .1x. Dense flight drawings use opt-in cubic Hermite interpolation of
positions and velocities with exact event-left states. They cannot restart
physics. The old cached continuous set remains selectable for comparison.

This does **not** establish continuous direct-surface scheduling throughput or
equivalent costs at the old cadence. Preparation measures a different, short
round trip. Human visual acceptance remains open. Once accepted, full-leg
steering families can become online solver seeds; any later cache should preserve
the accepted complete trajectory instead of forcing a circular staging orbit.
No new Astra review was run for this bounded comparison; the prior reviewed
ownership/funding architecture is reused.
