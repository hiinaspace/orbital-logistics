## A. Earth-Mars cyclers

Source key:
- BLA93 = Byrnes, Longuski, Aldrin, "Cycler orbit between Earth and Mars", J. Spacecraft & Rockets 30(3), May-June 1993, pp. 334-336, DOI 10.2514/3.25519. NTRS record: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930054986 (abstract only retrieved).
- RHL12 = Rogers, Hughes, Longuski, "Preliminary Analysis of Establishing Cycler Trajectories Between Earth and Mars via V-infinity Leveraging", AIAA 2012-4746, Aug 2012. https://engineering.purdue.edu/AAC/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/EstablishingCyclerTrajectoriesBetweenEarthAndMarsViaV-InfinityLeveraging-AIAA-2012-47461.pdf (PDF text read locally).
- CHEN06 = Chen, Okutsu, Landau, Longuski, "Low-Thrust Aldrin Cycler with Reduced Encounter Velocities", AIAA 2006-6021, Aug 2006. https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/low-thrust-aldrin-cycler-with-reduced-encounter-velocities/ (abstract only; V-infinity numbers below come from search-engine excerpts of the full paper, not read by me).

Facts:
1. Aldrin-cycler concept: a periodic Earth-Mars orbit lets a vehicle shuttle between the planets with moderate maneuvers at irregular intervals; a station on it could provide radiation shelter and living quarters; the orbit is largely maintained by Earth gravity assists. Numerical results cover 1995-2010. [sourced] BLA93 abstract, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930054986, 1993-06-01.
2. Earth-Mars synodic period about 2.135 yr (about 2.14 yr in Purdue papers). [sourced] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_cycler (secondary) and Purdue 2002-2006 excerpts via search. [inference] Check: 1/(1 - 1/1.881) = 2.135 yr from Mars' sidereal period 1.881 yr.
3. Aldrin cycler heliocentric orbit (circular coplanar model): semi-major axis 1.60 AU, e = 0.393, aphelion 2.23 AU, perihelion 0.97 AU; 2 vehicles. [sourced] RHL12 Table 1 (read in PDF text), Aug 2012.
4. Itinerary: Earth to Mars in 146 days, about 16 months beyond Mars' orbit, 146 days back to Earth's orbit. One eccentric loop per synodic period. [sourced] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_cycler (secondary); consistent with Purdue excerpts.
5. Period vs synodic period: a = 1.60 AU gives a heliocentric period of about 2.02 yr, which the Wikipedia article lists (with no reconciliation) against a 2.135 yr encounter cycle. [inference] 1.60^1.5 = 2.02 yr, so the vehicle period is slightly shorter than the 2.135 yr Earth-return interval. The mismatch (about 0.11 yr, roughly 40 degrees of Earth motion) is what the Earth flyby must absorb, i.e. the flyby rotates the apse line each cycle. It is not a clean integer resonance. Note the Wikipedia 41.9 degree turn figure fits this but is secondary.
6. Earth gravity assist is essential: RHL12 states that for some cycler classes a rotation of the apse line is required for repetition, and a cycler needing none is called ballistic. BLA93 says Earth gravity assists largely maintain the orbit. [sourced] RHL12 (Aug 2012); BLA93 (1993).
7. Small deterministic maneuvers: in the simple circular coplanar model, about 230 m/s per cycle (every 2 1/7 yr); in the full-ephemeris model, deep-space-maneuver needs varied 0 to 1.10 km/s over the 1995-2010 window. [sourced, secondary] A forum summary of BLA93: https://www.orbiter-forum.com/threads/the-aldrin-cycler.33504/ (not verified against the paper). Treat as unverified.
8. Hyperbolic excess speeds. Mars encounters are high: about 6 to over 12 km/s (earlier Purdue paper: about 7 to 12); Earth encounters about 5 to 7 km/s. Taxi propellant grows exponentially with Mars V-infinity (a fit of mass = 7281 exp(0.275 V-inf) appears in the 2006 paper). [sourced via search excerpts] CHEN06, AIAA 2006-6021 (full text not retrieved) and the 2002 Chen et al. powered-cycler paper (https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/preliminary-analysis-and-design-of-powered-earth-mars-cycling-tra/). A UMD student deck gives ballistic Mars arrival about 11 and 6 km/s for the "up" and "down" legs: https://pages.astro.umd.edu/~dphamil/HONR/Presentation/MacDonaldEthan_0.pdf (secondary, undated). Wikipedia (secondary) quotes taxi speeds of 6.7 km/s (Earth) and 9.8 km/s (Mars).
9. [inference] Consequence for a toy: the cycler flyby is not a free rendezvous. A taxi must burn to match an 11 km/s-class Mars flyby, so any honest "taxi docks with cycler" story has an expensive, high-relative-speed pass, not a gentle port approach.
10. Two vehicles: one on the outbound and one on the inbound cycler, offset by one synodic period, give a visit to each planet every synodic period (about 2.14 yr). [sourced] RHL12 Table 1 lists 2 vehicles for the Aldrin cycler (Aug 2012); the two-cycler wording is from Purdue excerpts via search. Aldrin's own page: https://buzzaldrin.com/?p=205 (not read).
11. Ballistic vs powered: ballistic cyclers use little propellant and can carry large vehicle mass; powered cyclers require large propellant expenditures; semi-cyclers make occasional stops. [sourced] RHL12 introduction, Aug 2012. The classic Aldrin cycler is treated as the simplest ballistic-style cycler (with the small maneuvers in 7). Powered/low-thrust Aldrin variants (CHEN06) lower encounter speeds at the price of cycler propellant.
12. Other families: VISIT-1/2 cyclers need 14 vehicles each (repeat every 7 synodic periods); two-synodic-period cyclers (Byrnes, McConaghy, Longuski 2002; S1L1, U0L1) need 4 vehicles but have lower encounter speeds. [sourced] RHL12 Table 1 (Aug 2012); NTRS 20060029853 (2002): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060029853. The "4 vehicles to 2 if one-way" remark is in RHL12.
13. Dimensionality: the published analyses use a circular coplanar model for design and ephemerides for validation (BLA93, RHL12). [inference] Real Mars has e = 0.093 and 1.85 degree inclination, so the real cycler is slightly 3D and the Earth flyby geometry (turn angle, closest approach 4,640 km below Earth radius in Wikipedia's simplified model, so a correction is needed) is essential.
14. [inference] A planar fixed-Sun toy where Earth is a massless rail cannot reproduce the gravity-assist-driven apse rotation. To get it, Earth must have mass (a patched-conic flyby or true n-body) and the cycler must be re-targeted each cycle.

## B. Artemis / Gateway / NRHO / DRO

### B1. Orbits
1. Gateway baseline: southern L2 NRHO in 9:2 resonance with the lunar synodic period (9 revolutions per two synodic months). Perilune radius about 3,200-3,550 km, apolune radius 70,000-72,000 km, designed to avoid Earth-shadow eclipses. [sourced] Davis, Khoury, Howell, Sweeney, "Phase control and eclipse avoidance in NRHOs", AAS paper (year not on retrieved text; NTRS record dates to about 2020): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200000733/downloads/20200000733.pdf (read in PDF text).
2. Same paper, 15-year propagation: period 6.26-6.76 d, mean 6.56 d; mean perilune radius 3,366 km (altitude 1,629 km); mean apolune radius 71,100 km (altitude 69,363 km). The orbit is "nearly stable" but needs regular orbit-maintenance maneuvers, with the x-axis-crossing control method, and phase control (perilune time can drift over 30 hours otherwise). [sourced] same URL.
3. Geometry: NRHO is part of the L2 halo family, an out-of-plane 3D periodic orbit of the circular restricted three-body problem (CR3BP), neither circular nor planar; its perilune passes over a lunar pole. [sourced] same paper ("part of the L2 halo orbit family"); pole statement from a secondary summary of Whitley and Martinez (2016), "Options for staging orbits in cislunar space" (not retrieved). [inference] Not representable in a planar model.
4. Artemis I (Nov-Dec 2022) used a lunar DRO: about 40,000 miles (about 64,000 km) beyond the Moon at farthest (NASA preview, 2022-04-18, https://www.nasa.gov/missions/orion-will-go-the-distance-in-retrograde-orbit-during-artemis-i/). Preview notes outbound flyby within about 60 miles, four major burns (outbound powered flyby, DRO entry, DRO departure, return powered flyby), and that retrograde means opposite the Moon's motion. NASA post-flight summary figure: 43,471 miles beyond the far side (about 70,000 km), 268,563 miles from Earth. [sourced] preview as above; post-flight figure via search summary of NASA's Artemis I summary, https://www.nasa.gov/reference/artemis-i-2/ (not read directly). Mission blogs give 57,000 miles for the farthest point and NASA does not reconcile this with 40,000 or 43,471 (different reference definitions are likely). [inference] ~70,000 km (post-flight) is the number to cite; the definition differs by source.
5. DROs are periodic orbits in the CR3BP that lie in the orbital plane (the planar symmetric family), and are highly stable. [sourced, partly] NASA calls the DRO "highly stable". [inference] Planarity is standard CR3BP knowledge, not stated on the NASA page; the JPL catalog lists the family as `dro` alongside `halo`.
6. JPL Three-Body Periodic Orbits catalog: https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/periodic_orbits.html (read 2026-10-07). Families include dro, halo, vertical, axial, lyapunov, resonant, butterfly, dragonfly, others; halo needs libr (L1/L2/L3) and a branch (N/S). Normalization: length unit = distance between primaries (km given as lunit), time unit = inverse of the system's mean angular rate (tunit in s), mass ratio mu = m2/(m1+m2) (Earth-Moon about 1.215e-2), synodic barycentric frame with +x toward the secondary, +z along angular velocity. Output rows: x,y,z,vx,vy,vz, Jacobi constant, period, stability index (stable if <= 1). [sourced]

### B2. Status as of 2026-10-07
Facts below are from NASA pages where noted; items I could not confirm are flagged.
1. Artemis II flew: launch 2026-04-01 (6:35 pm EDT, KSC 39B); free-return lunar flyby 2026-04-06 with closest approach 4,067 miles; farthest distance from Earth 252,756 miles (record); splashdown 2026-04-10 5:07 pm PDT off San Diego; crew Wiseman, Glover, Koch, Hansen; about 10 days. It was a free-return flyby, not an NRHO or DRO mission. ## C. What a planar impulsive toy can represent [inference]

A planar toy with circular-rail bodies, impulsive burns and a massless port can show faithfully: a DRO (a planar CR3BP periodic family, Artemis I's actual operating orbit; the 2D rotating-frame initial conditions come directly from the JPL catalog, provided the circular-restricted Earth-Moon model with its normalization is used, not prescribed rails alone); Hohmann-style shuttle legs between circular orbits, including an Earth-to-Mars Hohmann "taxi"; and a synthetic cycler (an ellipse with a=1.60 AU, e=0.393, 2.02 yr period) with a massless port, as long as the toy states it is a designed ellipse with scripted phasing and not a gravity-assist-maintained orbit. It cannot show: the NRHO (out of plane, perilune over a pole, 3D, needing the Moon's gravity in a restricted 3-body model plus out-of-plane dynamics); the real Aldrin-cycler apse rotation (Earth must have mass and flyby geometry, or the toy must script an apse rotation by hand at each Earth encounter); real Mars eccentricity (0.093) and inclination, so real launch windows and the actual 0 to 1.1 km/s DSM needs; or real ephemerides. Mars V-infinity of 6-12 km/s should be shown as a cost (the taxi's match burn), not a free docking. Artemis-era "status" (Gateway paused, Artemis III in LEO) means the NRHO is best presented as a documented concept or legacy plan rather than a live program.
