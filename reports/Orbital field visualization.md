# Orbital field visualization

Plan **ordinary-potential tint with sparse isolines for one known compact cycler exhibit first**, as an optional Canvas layer, off by default. It should explain the landscape behind freight while keeping journeys and arrivals dominant. A static rotating-basis cache gives exact cursor alignment without asynchronous epoch sampling. Add acceleration arrows independently only after the scalar layer reads well; defer effective potential, relief, LIC/advection and Solar coverage. The architecture supports a bounded display-only prototype, but overlay performance, readability and playtesting remain unmeasured. The adopted [Astra review disposition](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/potential-field/disposition.md) narrows the scope and fixes the cache contract; its verified source/code review ran as gpt-6-astra/high and included no experiments.

## A flat landscape preserves physical space

Potential per unit mass, **Φ = −Σμ/|r−b|**, provides the scalar landscape; its negative gradient provides gravitational acceleration. Neither includes ship thrust, support constraints or mutual ship gravity. An orbit also depends on velocity: a circular orbit's velocity is tangent while gravity points inward, which is why arrows cannot double as trajectory predictions. ([OpenStax: energy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/13-3-gravitational-potential-energy-and-total-energy), [satellite orbits](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/13-4-satellite-orbits-and-energy))

For the coplanar sets, a world-XY slice is the natural starting point. A restrained sequential lightness palette shows depth, while roughly 8–12 isolines provide shape and non-color cues. Use fixed per-set levels and an explicit compression convention; automatic normalization each frame can manufacture apparent changes during scrubbing. Mask actual body interiors before sampling and omit contour cells crossing them. Color compression is presentation, never gravity softening. Sequential lightness and redundant contour cues follow established scientific-color guidance. ([Matplotlib guide](https://matplotlib.org/stable/users/explain/colors/colormaps.html))

The strongest interaction precedent is **Falstad's vector-field simulator**, which combines potential coloring, equipotentials, arrows and flat/surface representations, and explicitly distinguishes particles following field velocity from particles responding to acceleration. Borrow the independent representations, with fewer simultaneous marks for the train-set viewer. Its separate electrostatics demo offers useful brightness/layer controls, but its documented line-charge law should not be imported as gravity. ([Vector simulator](https://www.falstad.com/vector2de/vector2de.html), [directions](https://www.falstad.com/vector/directions.html), [electrostatics directions](https://www.falstad.com/emstatic/directions.html))

A height surface can make wells and saddles immediately legible: ESA's effective-potential illustration is a useful orbital example, with an explicit warning that the picture alone does not establish equilibrium stability. But sheet height represents scalar energy, **not physical Z or spacetime curvature**. Real ships should retain their canonical positions; never make them ride the mesh. Nonlinear height compression also breaks proportionality between visible slope and acceleration. A separate analytical panel or clearly marked relief mode avoids fictitious occlusion of freight. ([ESA illustration](https://blogs.esa.int/rocketscience/2012/09/30/the-best-spot-for-space-astronomy/), [Einstein Online](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spotlight/geometry_force/))

## Rotating diagnostics need their own contract

The universal layer should show the instantaneous base field expressed in the selected view coordinates. Translating or rotating the camera/frame changes its presentation, not its scalar values or the physical sampling plane. It does not make those arrows the ship's full apparent acceleration: rotating dynamics include velocity-dependent Coriolis terms. ([MIT Press: rotating coordinates](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/9579/sicm_edition_2.zip/chapter001.html#%_sec_1.6.1))

The inspected compact force law fixes Aster at the origin and prescribes a circular Selene without an indirect term. In its origin-centered, moon-rate rotating frame, a derived effective potential is Φ_eff = Φ_g − ½n²(x²+y²). This is meaningful for **this prescribed field**, rather than the conventional barycentric CR3BP. Imported Lagrange-point coordinates or Jacobi values would describe a different model. A coast-only Jacobi-like quantity can be derived, but paid burns, capture and support change it. ([Compact physics](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/compact/physics.ts), [NASA/JPL coordinate convention](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/periodic_orbits.html), [MIT Press derivation](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/9579/sicm_edition_2.zip/chapter001.html#%_sec_1.8.4))

The official **heyoka CR3BP notebook** provides an inspectable grid-based permitted/forbidden-region map with integrated trajectories; Tellurion adds frame switching and a one-dimensional potential section. These suggest a later selected-coast diagnostic. Admit it only after checking source stationarity, frame policy, gradient parity and coast-invariant drift; disable unsupported ship/port frames. Convention detail: heyoka's helper `C` is half the conventional Jacobi constant and uses a different primary-axis convention, so its numerical thresholds cannot be copied. ([heyoka notebook](https://bluescarni.github.io/heyoka.py/notebooks/Periodic%20orbits%20in%20the%20CR3BP.html), [Tellurion playground](https://tellurion.dev/playgrounds/bsc-y2s1/AST2004-lagrange-points-cr3bp/index.html))

Solar coverage belongs later: the inspected heliocentric law subtracts origin acceleration, requiring a matching linear indirect-potential term; inclined, independently phased bodies do not admit one stationary rotating landscape. An arbitrary spatial slice also shows only its in-plane gradient. Those distinctions should live in capability metadata and optional details. ([Solar ephemerides and force law](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/solar/ephemeris.ts))

## Static direction serves traffic better than flow

A sparse, independently toggled acceleration-vector grid is the next useful option. Keep its sampling stable, compress magnitude explicitly, suppress near-zero vectors and avoid busy ship markers. It adds force direction without competing animated actors. If arrows look too diagrammatic, low-contrast static line-integral convolution is another research candidate: LIC filters texture along vector-field curves, providing dense orientation rather than inertial particle dynamics. Its direction sign remains ambiguous without occasional arrows. ([Cabral–Leedom original report](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1399414/))

Animated downhill dots solve a first-order flow equation; spacecraft solve a second-order equation from position **and velocity**. Label such animation “field flow” and keep it exploratory. Physical test particles would require initial velocities, bounded propagation/lifetimes, surface exclusion and explicit rewind/reseed semantics. They would remain separate from logistics resources. Falstad supplies the useful semantic distinction; it does not establish readability alongside this viewer's freight. ([Falstad directions](https://www.falstad.com/vector/directions.html))

## Canvas makes the first slice feasible

Code inspection found one Canvas 2D renderer, an orthographic camera and explicit frame transforms. A cached world-plane image can therefore use one affine projection; camera motion need not resample physics. Canvas supports transformed cached images, and OffscreenCanvas permits worker rendering. This establishes implementation feasibility, **not measured frame-budget headroom**. ([Renderer](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/main.ts), [camera](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/model-set/camera.ts), [Canvas transform](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/transform), [OffscreenCanvas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OffscreenCanvas))

Compact's sources rotate rigidly: **Φ(t,R(t)q)=Φ(0,q)**. Sample once in that static basis, then project **cached basis → world at cursor → selected frame → camera**. Cache identity includes field/basis/bounds/resolution/presentation settings, excluding cursor and view frame. Spatial approximation remains; epoch lag does not. Never advance the simulation frontier or derive sources from drawing interpolation. This exact reuse is specific to the verified compact field. ([Reviewed contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/potential-field/astra-review.md))

Use one coarse overview plus at most one bounded detail patch for close Moon zoom. Preserve covered coarse data while refinement runs and leave uncovered regions empty. Begin at 256² with a 512² sampling ceiling and bounded contour segments; one display worker owns one outstanding request plus replaceable desired bounds. Count pending/replaced buffers within **16MiB total field storage**. Detect cell–body intersections even when a body lies wholly inside a cell, and prevent filtered tint bleeding through excluded interiors. Suppress the layer and refinement near edge-on projection. These are implementation constraints, not established spatial accuracy.

Before acceptance, verify single-body contours, exterior force-gradient parity, rotational covariance, masks and paused/reverse/Live/frame-switch alignment; physical packets/resources must remain identical. Compare the whole viewer overlay on/off at 4K/16× with pan, close zoom, scrub and set switching. Provisional paired targets are **incremental total draw p95 ≤3ms and frame-gap p95 regression ≤5ms**, including refinement, uploads and contention. Historical measurements cover the old viewer only. ([Historical evidence](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/evidence/continuous-cycler-fleet.md:66))

The small timeline followup is independent: replace native vertical resize with a **top-edge separator** on the bottom-docked panel. Pointer capture adjusts height by upward drag, keeps the bottom fixed, clamps to 26px–30dvh and preserves lane scrolling; keyboard resizing and stopped event propagation prevent accidental scene orbit/scrub. ([Current CSS](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/style.css))
