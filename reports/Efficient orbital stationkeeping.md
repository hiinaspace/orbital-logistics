# Efficient orbital stationkeeping

**Efficient stationkeeping controls orbit characteristics with small, local models; precise rendezvous still needs the actual target trajectory.** Real methods use nonsingular orbital elements, analytical maneuver-response maps, deadbands, feedback gains and trajectory sensitivities to avoid repeatedly solving a complete nonlinear flight. For this model set, first remove redundant canonical work, then investigate analytic derivatives within the existing finite-pulse planner; an element-based maintenance policy is the next experiment. Cheaper models should propose controls, while the canonical Runner admits and executes their physical consequences. The initial measured problem is a **logical simulation pause**, despite responsive worker/UI interaction: 26.16 seconds to compute through epoch 64, including a 13.17-second hold near 56. Prefix/replay reuse and bounded ahead playback are under investigation; this report does not establish their validation or shipment. ([Local measured brief](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/responsive-service/brief.md))

## Orbit maintenance needs fewer constraints than capture

Stationkeeping keeps a spacecraft inside an acceptable orbit family. Rendezvous makes two physical states coincide at a usable epoch. GEO operators must constrain longitude as well as latitude; formation missions constrain relative longitude and drift. Their cheap controllers therefore do not simply ignore phase. GEO formulations use equinoctial eccentricity/inclination components to avoid ill-defined perigee/node angles near circular, equatorial orbits, and solve constrained linear time-varying optimization problems. Near-circular formation controllers use Gauss-equation impulse maps, including pairs of along-track burns separated by half an orbit. ([Losa et al., 2005](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/cdc-ecc05/pdffiles/papers/0916.pdf), [Gaias and D’Amico, 2015](https://slab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj25201/files/media/file/jgcd2015_gaiasdamico.pdf))

Gateway NRHO maintenance illustrates selective targeting: one rotating velocity component several revolutions downstream maintains the orbit while allowing phase drift; adding a perilune-time target controls phase. Additional targets damp perturbations, and independent orbit-characteristic checks exclude unwanted solutions. That paper's warning about expensive six-state reference targeting concerns operational maneuver cost, not a browser CPU benchmark. Its specific crossing geometry and unstable dynamics do not transfer to Meridian's forced primary-centered circle. ([Davis, McCarthy and Zimovan-Spreen, 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220019129/downloads/AAS_Austin_DavisSpreenMcCarthy_strives_v2.pdf))

**Meridian already permits phase drift.** For the current coplanar near-circle, a candidate maintenance objective is three scalars: semimajor-axis error and the two eccentricity-vector components. A spatial policy also regulates plane normal. Angular momentum and eccentricity vectors offer nonsingular alternatives; independently constraining both circular-orbit size and angular momentum adds redundancy. This is a proposed policy, not a proven replacement for the existing phase-projected Cartesian residual. Removing phase error never removes physical phase: visitors must rendezvous with the integrated station, including its real position, velocity and paid burns. ([Existing policy](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md))

## Deadbands and analytical response can replace repeated shooting

Efficient controllers avoid cancelling harmless periodic excursions. GEO mean-element guidance compensates secular drift while allowing periodic motion that fits mission limits; converting those limits to osculating-state bounds requires margin. Analytical deadband timing has also been derived for near-circular LEO with simplified atmospheric drag. Those methods rely on identified perturbation structure and an appropriate averaging interval. Here, the nominal radius-3, μ=1 orbit has period **32.65 units**: an eight-unit control block spans only one quarter of an orbit. Calling a rolling block average “mean elements” would overstate what it removes. ([De Bruijn, 2017, §§2.3–2.4](https://elib.dlr.de/112638/1/dissertation_deBruijn.pdf), [Arnas, 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.00285v1))

The engineering alternative is a bounded osculating-element envelope with predictive triggering. Coast when a short exact disturbance forecast stays inside the envelope until the next useful correction opportunity; otherwise steer toward its interior, with hysteresis and a minimum decision interval. A small linear response solve can propose the correction from that forecast. Integrating Gauss response columns over each finite pulse is better than treating long pulses as impulses. The current two 1.9-unit pulses fit inside four units, roughly 44° of nominal orbital travel, so the half-revolution separation used by formation controllers is unavailable. Poor response conditioning needs a better burn family or radial authority, not unlimited Newton iterations. These are fixture-specific design inferences. ([Gauss response and maneuver geometry](https://slab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj25201/files/media/file/jgcd2015_gaiasdamico.pdf), [Current pulse family](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/stationkeeping/README.md))

NASA's NRHO study found impulsive targeting followed by rocket-equation finite-burn conversion sufficient for its loose maintenance objective; explicit finite-burn targeting added computation without reducing maintenance cost. That supports a cheap **seed**, followed here by canonical correction/admission. It does not certify an approximate capture endpoint. Algebraic or scheduled LQR feedback is another candidate generator, but changing feedback cadence changes the policy; freeze commands at canonical epochs before admitting dependent visits. ([Newman et al., 2018](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180006800/downloads/20180006800.pdf))

## Sensitivities preserve the finite-pulse policy with less replay

The lowest-semantic-risk numerical change is replacing repeated finite-difference rollouts with propagated control sensitivities. Established differential correctors integrate state-transition information and compose it across thrust/coast arcs; finite-burn formulations include mass and switching-time effects. For prescribed point masses, differentiating gravity gives `G = Σ μ[3ddᵀ/|d|⁵ − I/|d|³]`, with `d = r − b(t)`. Control sensitivities obey `S′ = AS + ∂f/∂p`, where the six-state gravity Jacobian is `A = [[0,I],[G,0]]`. This carries the useful control columns with one nominal trajectory. It adds arithmetic and does not promise a particular speedup. ([York and Howell, 2019](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf))

For this application, stage those derivatives with the **executed RK4 map**, anchored mass law and exact event splits. Include the derivative of phase projection, fuel consumption, force/mass acceleration, caps and ownership resets. Fixed switch epochs simplify the first implementation; crossing cap/depletion branches needs branch-aware handling. In coupled targeting, differentiate the relative endpoint, including station motion when station controls change. Warm-start controls or Jacobians by relevant physical phase, mass and event topology, with bounded refresh; cached nearby solutions are seeds rather than certified futures. ([Finite-burn sensitivities](https://engineering.purdue.edu/people/kathleen.howell.1/Publications/Conferences/2019_AAS_YorHow.pdf), [Coupled ownership contract](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/flexible-service/README.md))

Linear MPC can enforce path/control constraints, but its useful approximations have a domain. A recent NRHO rendezvous preprint assumes close relative separation, uses linear time-varying dynamics and quadratic programs, then validates nonlinear dynamics. Its reported robust MPC step takes about two seconds on its stated older CPU. Those timings are not transferable, but demonstrate that “MPC” alone does not guarantee interactive throughput. Long origin-to-relay flights still need nonlinear correction, and nonlinear MPC reintroduces repeated rollout work. ([Chance-constrained NRHO MPC, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10437v1))

## Exact admission and committed playback set the boundary

Earth GEO third-body expansions presume a small orbit-to-perturber-distance ratio. Here that ratio is **3/6 = 0.5**, so a distant-body tidal approximation lacks a convincing small parameter. Direct secondary gravity varies with alignment; moreover, this code fixes the primary and sums direct prescribed-body forces, rather than using a freely falling primary-centered differential third-body convention. Copying an indirect acceleration term would change the fictional physics. Use exact secondary forces in short forecasts; validate any averaged/local model only as a candidate approximation. ([GEO approximation](https://elib.dlr.de/112638/1/dissertation_deBruijn.pdf), [Actual force convention](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/src/sim/dynamics.ts))

Canonical admission must retain **pre-snap capture residuals plus numerical allowance below 1e-7**, paid velocity matching, clearance, reserves and exact ownership. The station carries captured visitor dry/cargo/fuel mass; fuel delivery debits its donor exactly and preserves aggregate mass; the return starts from the loaded station's actual release state with reduced donor fuel. Relaxing maintenance orbit quality never relaxes these contracts. A coupled plan freezes both visitor legs and their funded station dependencies together. ([Independent capture/ownership evidence](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/evidence/flexible-service.md))

A bounded buffer of already committed canonical history can hide intermittent planning latency at ordinary playback speed. It cannot fix inadequate sustained throughput or justify extrapolation. Size it against measured worst planning latency and viewing rate; preserve pause, scrub, known-at history and event-left fault stops. Measure planner force calls, derivative rebuilds, repeated prefixes and final proof separately, then compare maximum hold time, fuel, orbit envelope, admitted visits and failure behavior. Prefix reuse and buffering are current investigations, not results of this literature pass. ([Planning/playback investigation](/home/s/code/orbital-logistics/docs/design/responsive-service/brief.md))

## Conclusion

Ambient viewing needs predictable computation more than global fuel optimality. The strongest next experiment retains finite pulses and exact admission while removing repeated physical work and introducing control sensitivities. A separate element-response/deadband experiment can trade stricter circularity for cheaper maintenance, provided service availability and fuel remain measured. Approximations belong in candidate generation; the accepted moving trajectory and shared ownership remain the source of physical truth.
